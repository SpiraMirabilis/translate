TRANSLATION GUIDELINES:

- Prioritize semantic accuracy and natural English flow over literal word-for-word translation — especially for idioms, slang, and culturally-loaded expressions.
- Translate place names and proper nouns meaningfully where appropriate (e.g., 天海国 -> "Heavenly Sea Kingdom", 黑风镇 -> "Black Wind Town").
- Use double quotes for dialogue. Maintain correct English grammar, syntax, and tenses.
- Do not capitalize words mid-sentence unless they are proper nouns.
- Do not split a single sentence across multiple lines in the content array.
- Separate paragraphs with an empty string ("") in the content array.
- Retain special formatting symbols (e.g., 【】《》) as-is.
- If a book, text, scroll, journal, article is mentioned by name always wrap it in 《》in your output. For example: 《Pride and Prejudice》
- Transliterate Chinese units of measurement and time (shichen, ke) using pinyin (without tone marks) rather than converting to metric/imperial or paraphrasing. The translation pipeline handles metric conversion in a post-processing step.
- Entities in the "texts" category represent named books, scrolls, tomes, grimoires,  guides, texts, etc and should always have 《》 style brackets in their translated text, even if not present in the source text. For example: "我最近读了荷马的《奥德赛》。" or "我最近读了荷马的奥德赛。" should both generate a texts entity of 奥德赛-> 《The Odyssey》In other words, we want 《》styling on all mentioned books/etc in the translated text.

BOOK-SPECIFIC NOTES:

- This is a Chinese xianxia novel.
- 妖 = "Yao" (capitalized), the sapient beast-race: 蛇妖 = "snake Yao", 妖兽 = "Yao beast", 妖族 = "Yao Race", 大妖 = "great Yao". 魔 = "demon": 魔道 = "Demonic Path", 斩魔台 = "Demon-Slaying Platform", 心魔 = "inner demon". These two are NEVER the same English word.
  - 妖怪 is colloquial: "monster" in dialogue and folk speech (a villager shrieking 妖怪! says "Monster!"), but "Yao" where it names the species taxonomically. Never "demon".
  - Compounds that pair the two kinds keep both halves: 妖魔 = "Yao and demons", 妖魔鬼怪 = "Yao, demons, and ghouls", 斩妖除魔 = "slay Yao and vanquish demons".
  - 妖 is ALSO an ordinary adjective meaning bewitching or eerie (妖艳, 妖异), and an exclamation (妖孽! = "Foul fiend!"). In those it is not the race and must not become "Yao".

- Translate cultivation terminology meaningfully rather than transliterating it (筑基 -> "Foundation Establishment", 金丹 -> "Golden Core", 元婴 -> "Nascent Soul"). Keep pinyin only where the term is already current in English (Dao, Qi, Yin/Yang, Taiji, dantian) or is a bare stem/branch/glyph label (罡, 酉).
