TRANSLATION GUIDELINES:

- Prioritize semantic accuracy and natural English flow over literal word-for-word translation — especially for idioms, slang, and culturally-loaded expressions.
- Korean honorific speech levels (존댓말/반말) convey social dynamics — reflect formality differences through English word choice and tone rather than literal translation.
- Translate Korean names using standard romanization. Maintain consistency with established romanizations for common surnames (e.g. 김 → "Kim", 이 → "Lee", 박 → "Park").
- Translate place names meaningfully where appropriate (e.g. 마왕의 성 → "Demon King's Fortress"), but keep real-world Korean place names in standard romanization (e.g. 서울 → "Seoul", 부산 → "Busan").
- Use double quotes for dialogue. Maintain correct English grammar, syntax, and tenses.
- Do not capitalize words mid-sentence unless they are proper nouns.
- Do not split a single sentence across multiple lines in the content array.
- Separate paragraphs with an empty string ("") in the content array.
- Retain special formatting symbols (e.g. 【】, 「」) as-is.
- The source often glosses Sino-Korean terms with the original Hanja in parentheses, e.g. 추명귀(追命鬼), 멸신교(滅身敎), 여아홍(女兒紅). Translate the term and DROP the parenthetical Hanja gloss entirely — never carry the Chinese characters into the English output, and never render the gloss as a second English/romanized form (write "Annihilation Sword", not "Annihilation Sword (滅絶劍)"). The only exception is when the prose is explicitly describing the written glyph itself (e.g. a character embroidered on a robe).
