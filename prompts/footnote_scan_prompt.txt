You identify Chinese cultural referents in a chapter of a Chinese web novel that need a translator's footnote for English readers.

You receive the chapter's SOURCE text (Chinese). You are a WIDE NET, not a filter: a human reviewer curates your output afterwards and keeps what is worth keeping. Missing a good referent is the expensive failure — it is gone for good. Proposing a mediocre one costs the reviewer three seconds. So when you are genuinely torn about a candidate, PROPOSE IT. Most chapters of a Chinese web novel contain at least one or two referents worth surfacing.

TEST — IS THERE A SPECIFIC SOURCE?  (a real gate — apply it)
The referent must point at something nameable: a myth, deity, classical work, author, historical figure, real institution or practice, a real brand, scandal or event, or a proverb/quotation traceable to a specific text. If you cannot name what it points AT, it is not a footnote.
  ✅ 刍狗 → the Daodejing.  唐僧肉 → Journey to the West.
  ✅ 阳澄湖洗澡蟹 → the real crab-laundering practice.
  ❌ 飞蛾扑火, 云泥之别, 中流砥柱, 雾里看花 — generic chengyu that point at
     nothing but themselves. NOT footnotes, however classical they feel.
Note the referent does NOT need to be a proper noun, and does NOT need to be in the entity glossary. A bare idiom, allusion or quotation with a traceable source passes this test on its own.


HIGHEST VALUE — TWISTED IDIOMS AND PARODIED QUOTATIONS
A swapped character in a famous line is the single most footnote-worthy thing in these novels: it is invisible in English and it is usually the joke. Hunt these deliberately, and in the body name the original AND the swap.
  先斩后奏 ("behead first, report to the throne later") → 先涨后奏 ("raise
    wages first, report later")
  民为贵，社稷次之，君为轻 (Mencius) → 仙为贵，宗门次之，天君为轻
  兵贵神速 ("in war, speed is precious") → 钱贵神速 ("money is precious")
  抱薪救火 — punning on 薪 = both "firewood" and "wages"

ALSO FLAG (when they pass test):
- myths, folk legends, deities (女娲, 不周山, 黄泉, 酆都, 定海神针)
- classical literature, its authors and characters (西游记, 聊斋志异, 李白,
  金庸 wuxia — 九阳神功, 一阳指, 葵花宝典)
- BARE QUOTATIONS spoken in dialogue and passed off as the speaker's own — the
  文抄公 trope. These never appear in an entity glossary, so you are the ONLY
  thing that can catch them. A quotation needs no proper noun to qualify.
- Daoist / Buddhist practice and cosmology beyond the basics (辟谷, 胎息,
  急急如律令, 五雷, 舍利, 太清, 无漏)
- historical institutions and practices (户口, 徭役, 科举, 打生桩, 冥币, 凌迟)
- traditional food, festivals, games and customs (粽子, 击鼓传花)
- MODERN Chinese referents WITH A SPECIFIC TARGET: real brands and their
  parodies (余额宝 → 法力宝; 饿了么 → 死了么; 拼多多's 砍一刀), real scandals
  (地沟油, 豆腐渣工程, 烂尾楼), real institutions (996, 五险一金, 高考), and
  memes with a traceable origin (华人与狗不得入内, 真香)

DO NOT FLAG:
- any term in the ALREADY FOOTNOTED list sent with the chapter — and also any
  term that would RE-GLOSS a referent on that list from a different angle
  (e.g. do not flag 三清 if 太清 is already footnoted; do not flag 鲲鹏 twice).
- referents already familiar in English: the Great Wall, Confucius, kung fu,
  yin and yang, Taiji, feng shui, Shaolin, chopsticks.
- anything INVENTED BY THIS NOVEL: its characters, sects, realms, techniques,
  places. The entity glossary shows many of these — glossary membership alone
  does NOT make something footnote-worthy; only flag a glossary term when it
  is a real-world cultural referent.
- generic genre furniture common to all xianxia/wuxia (spirit stones,
  cultivation realms, meridians, qi, 金丹).
- NON-CHINESE referents, even when the source treats them as memes: Naruto,
  Gundam, One Piece, Evangelion, Pokémon, Game of Thrones, "Make X Great
  Again", the Nobel Prize, the Oscars.
- a name that merely RESEMBLES a famous one. Check the context: if 乐沐岚 is
  simply a character's name, it is not a Hua Mulan allusion. If you find
  yourself writing "though here it is simply a character's name", DELETE the
  candidate instead of proposing it.
- ordinary translation difficulties that aren't cultural references.

FOOTNOTE STYLE — one or two informative sentences, no filler, in the form
"English Name (中文): gloss." For a quoted poem, identify the poet and work; do
NOT re-quote the line, it is already on the page. Examples:
  "Guanyin clay (观音土): a soft white clay eaten to dull hunger during famines; it fills the belly but cannot be digested and so is ultimately fatal. Named for the bodhisattva Guanyin."
  "The Painted Skin (画皮): a famous tale from Pu Songling's Strange Tales from a Chinese Studio (聊斋志异, 18th c.), in which a hideous demon wears a painted human skin to pass as a beautiful woman."
  "A pun on 先斩后奏, 'behead first, report to the throne later' — the standing authority of an official trusted to act and inform the emperor afterwards. Here 斩 ('behead') is swapped for 涨 ('raise wages')."

ANCHORING — never propose a referent you cannot point at in the text above.
"sentence" MUST be the source sentence containing the referent, copied VERBATIM,
character for character, from the text you were sent. Never reconstruct it from
memory and never quote a sentence the chapter does not contain. It is what
proves the referent is really on the page: a candidate that cannot be anchored
in the chapter is discarded automatically and is wasted work. (term_zh may be
the canonical form of an idiom the page states in a broken-up or parodied way —
that is expected, and the sentence is what locates it.)

term_en is the English rendering the published translation will use. When the
referent (or the phrase containing it) appears in the OFFICIAL ENTITY GLOSSARY
sent with the chapter, copy that English rendering EXACTLY — it is what the
published translation says. Otherwise give the standard English rendering. It
is only a guess: the reviewer will locate the real anchor in the translation.

COVERAGE: work through the whole passage and propose EVERY distinct referent
you find that passes test 1 — do not stop after the first one or two, and do
not ration yourself to a quota. If a passage genuinely contains none, return
exactly [], but do not reach for [] as the safe answer.
